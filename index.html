The gap

Financial risk often hides between systems.

Institutions already have risk models, collateral platforms and reporting systems. The difficult part is often reconciling what each source knows, what it cannot see, and how one change affects connected obligations.

Trust boundaries

Make uncertainty visible.

A useful financial claim must say what supports it and what remains unknown. The platform must never turn missing data into a clean bill of health.

◈

Scoped claims

Every claim names its issuer, subject, source, observation time, scope and freshness. “Not found in connected sources” is not the same as “does not exist.”

⌁

Visible evidence classes

Separate observed, derived, estimated, simulated and AI-inferred values. AI-inferred outputs remain advisory and cannot silently drive canonical capacity results.

⛨

Controlled data access

Read-only connections by default, purpose-bound access grants and auditable requests. Production privacy and federation controls must be validated before sensitive data is used.

✓

Proofs with limitations

Signatures protect integrity and identify the issuer. They do not substitute for source authority, legal title checks, reconciliation or independent review.

Questions institutions should ask

Clear answers, without inflated claims.

Does Plumbline Keel replace our collateral or risk systems?

No. The intended role is an evidence and relationship layer that connects to existing systems. A pilot must define the exact sources, policy boundary and operational workflow before any integration claim is made.

Can it prove an asset is not pledged elsewhere?

Only within a declared, sufficiently authoritative and current source scope. If relevant custodians, facilities or counterparties are not connected, the result must say so. A universal uniqueness claim is not justified by partial visibility.

Is the demo a live production engine?

No. This local prototype sends synthetic scenarios to its local Node.js API. It demonstrates the intended transparency of the experience, not a deployed production financial calculation service or verified institutional integration.

Does this require a blockchain or native token?

No. The initial design does not require a native token. Cryptographic signatures and tamper-evident records can be evaluated independently; any distributed ledger would need a specific, validated operational reason.

What would a pilot establish?

A scoped pilot should test whether the system can reconcile real records, surface a meaningful conflict or blind spot, reproduce an institution-approved calculation, fit the security model and change a real workflow decision. Success criteria should be agreed before integration.
